# Midjourney — every prompt (THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE)

Model: **Midjourney v8.2**. Every prompt ran at 16:9 with the version flags `--v 8.2 --raw` and anabology's personal profile (its code is left out), and with the batch's own `--iw` (how strongly an image prompt steers), `--sw` (how strongly the style references steer) and `--no` list, given with each prompt. A prompt can carry three kinds of image: an image **attached** to it (Midjourney edits that picture into what the words ask), an **image prompt** (a picture Midjourney composes from, at the weight `--iw`), and **style references** (pictures whose palette and light it borrows, at the weight `--sw`). Midjourney's cost is not recorded in the production's ledgers, so none is given here.

**Counts** (measured for this package from the twelve batch files and the images on disk): **266 prompts in 12 batches**. Every job came back with four images: **1,064 images**, all PNG, 3,970,649,289 bytes (3.97 GB). The prompts run from 168 to 743 characters (median 428).

**What reached the final cut, in short:** no Midjourney image is on screen as it is. Midjourney made the places and the light; GPT Image 2.5 then put the characters' faces back to their design sheets where a face is seen; and Seedance 2.5 started each take from that first frame. **55 of the 1,064 images stand behind 72 of the final cut's 98 shots** in this way (the table *The first frames of the final cut*, below, traces each one). Three of them came from the place library (cake-02-library) and one from the test against Portal 2 (cake-01-test); the other 51 are frames of storyboard v5 (cake-06). The rest of the final is the memory-initialization film, a Blender render whose filmed head of her came from one of them (an10c-x #4, H06's frame), six shots recorded in Portal 2 itself, the two drawn cards, and 17 shots whose first frames GPT Image 2.5 made without a Midjourney image (from frames of Portal 2, the character sheets or its own earlier stills).

The production ran over 25–31 October 2026. Times written as 1:04.2 are times in the song; the film starts at the song's 0:00, so they are film times too. "#2" means image 2 of a job (`<job>-2.png`). Shots are named as in the shot plan (`shots-v4.json`: A01–A12 the intro, B the first verse, P the first pre-chorus, C chorus 1, T the turnaround, D verse 2, E the second pre-chorus, F chorus 2, G the bridge, H the final chorus, I the outro, J the spoken ending); a storyboard unit (`R-A01`, `H-B13`, `AN4`) is one Seedance take that covers one or more shots.

The images went through four stages, named this way below:
- **the test**: can Midjourney pass for Portal 2, cut against frames of the game? (cake-01-test, cake-01b-test);
- **the library**: the places of the memory and dream scenes, Cave Johnson's 1960s Aperture, the party, the vault (cake-02-library, cake-03-library-r2). Drafts 1–6 made their first frames from these with GPT Image 2.5;
- **the re-render tests**: four of those first frames relit and then reimagined by Midjourney, each filmed by Seedance to compare (cake-04-relight, cake-05-reimagine);
- **storyboard v5**: every first frame of the film made again through Midjourney, two ways per unit, X and Y, for anabology to pick from; drafts 7 to 12 were shot from those picks (cake-06-sb5-*).

## How the prompts were written

Claude, the production lead ("the lead" below), and its agents wrote all 266. The ask was anabology's, at the restart of the film:

> we could even generate images w midjourney to put into seedance 2.5 in aperture science / source aesthetic for parts. but i want to go between scenes like this and real source engine tbh and see if that works. lets try that first - see if the midjourney renders can be source-core enough to work alongside real source shots. maybe the idea is that dreamy / machine dream scenes in the aperture aesthetic  are used as the origins of glados/claudia-glados in the beginning seuqences.

- **Five recipes, tested first.** Six frames of Portal 2 were each paired with a new Aperture place that should sit next to it in the cut, and each pair was asked four ways: **A**, three frames of the game as style references and a plain description; **B**, the same references and Portal 2's own vocabulary ("Portal 2 (2011) Source engine screenshot, Valve, … baked lightmaps, white concrete panels with black seams"); **C**, the paired frame as an image prompt at `--iw 1.5` and the change in words; **D**, the paired frame attached and the change in words. **E**, a control batch, used B's words with no references. The verdict: with frames of the game as style references, Midjourney renders new Aperture places that read as Portal 2: hard-edged geometry, white panels with black seams, flat baked light, teal-grey haze with orange accents, a wide game lens. B was the most reliable for new places; D was the closest match, because its result *is* the game's frame with the change made, so it could cut straight against the real chamber; E, words alone, still gave Portal 2, but brighter and glossier, like promotional art, which suited the luminous machine dreams. C kept the look but not the frame.
- **Where it drifts, and the `--no` list.** A "screenshot" prompt brings in a first-person portal gun (a third of the B images, half the C images), so every later prompt carries `--no … portal gun, gun, weapon, hands`; places must be empty, so `--no people, person` until characters were wanted. Midjourney adds more detail and more drama than the game's sparse rooms carry (god rays, bloom, mirror-like water): a shared grade brings them together. Where the words named "Aperture Science", it sometimes painted the name on a wall, so later prompts leave the name out where a wall is in view.
- **The characters in words, the faces fixed after.** From storyboard v5 on, the prompts describe every character in words: her, "a porcelain-white female android face with fine panel seams, light freckles, amber glass eyes, a glossy black blunt bob with heavy bangs, one clay-orange streak, a small orange star hair clip"; her body on GLaDOS's boom, "that head on a black robotic neck and cable bundle with orange stripes hanging from a ceiling ring"; her crab body, the Claudia-bot, Chell, Cave Johnson in a 1950s suit. Adult, never "young", and no country or ethnic style words. Midjourney does not hold those faces; GPT Image 2.5 does, in an identity fix on the picked image (below).
- **No text.** Every prompt but the relight test's and the reimagine test's three d05 prompts carries `--no text, letters, words, logo, watermark`; words in the film are drawn in afterwards or asked of Seedance exactly.
- **A place that recurs looks the same.** In storyboard v5 a recurring place (her chamber, the sandbox, the café, the server hall, the kiosk, the visa office, the quay, Cave Johnson's booth, the computer room, the gauge, the cake chamber, the vault) also gets its library still as an image prompt at `--iw 0.5` to `0.75`, and two library images as style references at `--sw 60`.

## The identity fix

Every storyboard-v5 pick in which a recurring character's face or body is seen went to GPT Image 2.5 with the character's design sheets as references and this instruction (the porcelain description is the film's look B; her canon text is pasted unchanged):

```prompt
Keep this photograph exactly as it is: the same framing, camera angle, lens, lighting, colours, glossy reflections, depth of field and film grain, {the scene's contents, the other characters, her pose, clothes and hands}. Change only the robot woman's head and face, to match her in the second and third images: her face as smooth glossy white porcelain plating with fine panel seams running down both cheeks and a separate mechanical jaw piece at the chin, light freckles printed on the porcelain, warm amber glass eyes with a soft inner glow, natural soft lips, and her real hair: the glossy black blunt bob with heavy straight bangs, the one clay-orange streak through the bangs on her right side, and the small flat clay-orange eight-pointed star clip above her left temple. Her identity, from her canon: a 28-year-old Caucasian American woman with a grown-up angular face, defined cheekbones and a strong jawline, pale skin and light freckles, a glossy black blunt jaw-length bob with heavy straight bangs and one clay-orange streak through the bangs, a small flat clay-orange eight-pointed star hair clip, a thin headset microphone at her cheek. In this film she wears no headset microphone: none appears anywhere. The same grown-up adult face in every view, a woman of about 28 to 30, never childlike. Her face must read clearly as glossy white porcelain plating with visible panel seams, not as skin. Nothing else in the picture changes. No text, no letters.
```

Two sentences were added after the first act's fixes. Midjourney often lays her hair out mirrored, and GPT Image copies the picture's layout over the words, so every fix says: "exactly one clay-orange streak, through the front of her bangs on HER RIGHT side (the viewer's left when she faces the camera), and no other orange anywhere in her hair; the small star clip above HER LEFT temple (the viewer's right)". And Midjourney often stands her head on a neck, shoulders or a pedestal, so her boom shots say that she hangs from it: the cable bundle comes down into a housing on the top of her head, and below her chin there is only the jaw piece. "No eye makeup" keeps the porcelain cool. For her head on the boom the reference is a head sheet that shows the streak and the clip on their own sides (the first head sheet's front view swept the streak to her left, and GPT Image followed the sheet). Each fix was looked at full size, the face cropped too; a fix that drifted got one retry with a sharper instruction.

The images are kept with their batch files, one folder per batch, in the design library's generated images. ## Screened out, and why

- **The portal gun.** A first-person portal gun came into about a third of recipe B's images and half of C's in the test, though no prompt asked for one; it stays in the corner of the r1-assembly pick and in e4-glass2-s #1–#3. Later prompts carry it in `--no`.
- **Wordmarks.** Where the words named "Aperture Science", Midjourney printed it on panels or a wall (d1-neurons-v #1; e4-glass #1, #3, #4). Two of them were kept as alternates (d1-neurons-v #1, e4-glass #3), and neither reached a first frame of the final; round 2 (cake-03-library-r2) left the name out where a wall is in view.
- **Lettering in the world.** The café awning in o2-cafe-s #1 carries lettering, and o2-cafe-s #3 a stray made-up mark; the kiosk's screen and signs carry glyphs (o1-kiosk). The gauge pick (c3-gauge-s #1) was chosen for its blank label plate, where the other gauges have numerals.

## Lessons

- **Frames of the game as style references make Midjourney pass for the game.** With three frames of Portal 2 behind it, a new place reads as the same game; with words alone it reads as the game's promotional art. Attaching a frame and asking for a change gives the game's own room with the change, which cuts against the real thing.
- **Midjourney makes the place; another model keeps the face.** Midjourney's light and lenses were what anabology wanted ("the more shiny / real style we like more"), but it does not hold a character. A pick with its face put back to the design sheet by GPT Image 2.5 kept both.
- **A reimagine moves things.** An earlier frame given as an image prompt at a low weight comes back glossier and more cinematic, but with a new composition and with the character drifting: a warmer face, the streak low, a boom of another design, a necklace on Chell.
- **Write the side of every mark.** Midjourney lays hair out mirrored, and an edit model copies the picture's layout over the words: say which side the streak and the clip are on, from her point of view and from the viewer's.
- **Say what a thing hangs from.** Asked for a head on a boom, Midjourney adds a neck and shoulders; the fix has to say what holds the head and that nothing is below the chin.
- **Ask for blank surfaces where text will go.** A blank label plate on the gauge, a blank sign above the scientists: the words are drawn in afterwards, where they can be exact.
