# Midjourney — every prompt (AIRPLANE MODE)

Model: **Midjourney v8.2**. Every prompt ran with `--ar 16:9 --v 8.2 --raw --hd` and anabology's personal profile (its code is left out); no prompt had any other parameter, so none are shown after the prompts. 55 prompts carry style references and 35 carry image prompts (Midjourney's Styles and Images slots); they are named under the prompt as "image references". Two prompts were re-run in Midjourney's new thinking mode. The moving inserts were made from these images with **Midjourney Animate** (its own section, below).

**Counts** (measured for this package from the job files and the images on disk): **149 prompts in 25 batches**, am-01 to am-24 plus am-15b. 135 jobs came back with images: **548 images**, the 540 of those jobs plus 8 from the two thinking-mode re-runs, all 2944×1648 PNG, 3,353 MB in total. 14 jobs have no images: eleven prompts over 1,000 characters that Midjourney rewrote on submission (am-01 to am-03), two in am-05 for no recorded reason, and one job whose images are missing (am-18 w-S20). **82 images became plates or references** that reached a delivered cut; 27 more were picked and then not used.

There are two delivered cuts. **The first cut** (v2) went out; anabology had asked to see nothing before it. **The final film** (v3) went out, after anabology's notes on the first cut. Batches am-01 to am-14 made the first cut's pictures, am-15 to am-24 the final film's. "Both cuts" below means the image's use survived into the final film.

The kinds after each job name come from the job files: `plate` is a still for the real world (the cabin, the story, the exteriors) or a Seedance first frame; `lp` is the first cut's low-poly VR world; `ref` is a reference image for the final film's avatar. "#2" means image 2 of that job (`<job>-1.png` to `<job>-4.png`). After a prompt, "plate: #2" marks the picked image and what it became; "not used" marks a pick that never reached a delivered cut; "anabology: comment on #2" or "reject #2" marks the director's words on a shot built from that image (quoted at the end). The production lead picked every plate; anabology did not rank these images.

## How the prompts were written

Claude (the lead session) wrote every prompt, from the storyboard and the research. Her identity is text alone, as on the earlier films: Midjourney v8 has no character reference, so a fixed canon goes into every prompt of her. The canon blocks, as they appear in the prompts:

- **Her, close and medium shots:** "a Caucasian American woman in her late twenties with a grown-up angular face, defined cheekbones and a strong jawline, pale skin with light freckles across her nose, dark calm eyes, a glossy black blunt jaw-length bob with heavy straight bangs to the brows and one clay-orange streak through the bangs, a small flat clay-orange eight-pointed star hair clip, a thin black boom headset microphone curving along her cheek to the corner of her mouth". "Grown-up" and "angular" are there on purpose: on the earlier films anabology rejected plates where she read young, so every plate of her was screened for age and for ethnicity (Midjourney drifts her ethnicity) before use.
- **Her, full length or far away:** "a Caucasian American woman in her late twenties with pale skin and a glossy black blunt bob with heavy straight bangs and one clay-orange streak, a thin black headset microphone". The full text makes Midjourney crop to her face.
- **One look, all film** (the flight is one night): "wearing a couture flight suit: a glossy international-orange satin jumpsuit with a silver front zip, thin silver reflective piping along the sleeves and a wide white leather belt, the sleeves pushed up to the elbow".
- **The opener and the cabin:** "Jet-set high fashion editorial, a couture magazine story shot on a night flight:" and "inside a modern widebody airliner cabin at night, deep indigo and violet LED mood lighting along the ceiling, navy-blue leather seats with thin red piping, glowing seat-back screens, oval windows". The cabin is navy and violet, like Delta's, but no prompt names Delta or any other brand.
- **The low-poly world** (am-01 to am-04): "a low-poly 3D video game render, flat-shaded chunky polygons, low-resolution painted textures, crisp aliased edges, bright saturated cyan sky with low-poly cumulus clouds, toon lighting with hard shadows, a strong fisheye lens, Dreamcast and PlayStation 2 era game art", with frames of the reference video anabology chose (Nam Mac's "SLUMLORDZ ~ SANDWICH", 2026) as style references.
- Every prompt ends "no text, no letters, no logos": all type in the film is drawn in code.

**Length.** Midjourney rewrites prompts past about 1,000 characters into its own wording, and this record has no images from the rewritten jobs. Eleven of the twelve prompts over 1,000 characters (1,065 to 1,210) have no images here; the twelfth, at 1,013, does. From am-05 on the canon was compacted ("a Caucasian American woman in her late twenties with a grown-up angular face, pale freckled skin, a glossy black blunt bob with heavy bangs and one clay-orange streak, a small clay-orange star hair clip, a thin black headset microphone") and prompts stayed under about 750 characters. The median prompt is 637 characters; the shortest is 194.

**The rules that changed the wording.** Three came from screening the plates and the takes:
- **Adult.** Where a plate or a take read young, the re-still said so outright: "in her early thirties with an angular adult face" (am-16) and, for the blocked escort, "a 32-year-old grown woman with elegant mature features" (am-19).
- **One suit, one headset.** The same orange suit in every shot, and from am-08 one headset: a white shell, a glossy black visor and a clay-orange strap. The picked headset plate went in as an image prompt where the headset had drifted to ski goggles or a clear visor.
- **The guards.** "guards in matte-black tactical gear and black helmets with mirrored visors hiding their faces, plain white chest patches, black gloves". No prompt names Anthropic; the real wordmark and symbol were set on the patches in code, tracked to each take. When the white of the headset and the patches bled into white suits on two takes, the re-stills showed only black sleeves and gloves.

**Thinking mode.** anabology's brief: "midjourney now has a thinking mode for higher coherence btw, can experiment with some withand some without." Two jobs were re-run with "Rerun (Thinking)" on the same prompt and references: the avatar turnaround (am-01 lp-her-sheet-i) and the singing visor (am-05 s-visor-sing). Thinking held what the prompt asked: all four views of the turnaround in every image, the headset mic on every view and the eight-pointed star clip (the normal run drew five-pointed stars and lost the headset in half), and a real VR headset in all four cabin images (the normal run drifted to ski-goggle visors in three of four). It cost variety between the four, softer hands and a stronger purple cast. The lead's conclusion: thinking for references that must hold, normal for exploring. No thinking-mode image was used.

## What the plates became

The lead picked one image per plate from contact sheets. The picks file itself is not included; the picks below come from the scripts and clip records that used them (`tools/v3takes.py`, `tools/vrsd.py`, `tools/vrsd2.py`, `tools/vrsd3.py`, the Animate lists, the chapter files), and the first cut's Seedance first frames were identified by matching each take's first frame against the batches. Where a pick cannot be traced to one image, the annotation says so.

- **Seedance first frames.** Every story and cabin clip of her starts from a Midjourney still (the clips and their prompts are in [prompts-seedance.md](https://claudia.gallery/record/airplane-mode/prompts-seedance/)). The final film's 17 new story clips start from stills of am-16 to am-24; nine clips of the first cut, from am-02, am-05, am-08 to am-10, am-12 and am-14, carried over into the final film.
- **The VR world.** In the first cut, the low-poly stills of am-01 and am-03 were the style references (@Image1) for Seedance restyles of Blender motion. In the final film, am-18's stills of the avatar in each shot's world were @Image3 of the avatar replacements, and am-15b av2-front #4 was the avatar herself.
- **Midjourney Animate.** 34 images were animated and 33 came back; 13 animations play in the first cut and 7 in the final film, and one more lends its camera motion to its still in both (see below).
- **Stills in the film.** The phone (am-13 s-tap-night #1), the morning overhead panel (am-07 s-psu-morning #1), the VEND tile (am-09 lp-her-vend-adult #2), THE ESCAPE's key art (am-15b av2-front #4) and six of am-18's stills as the levels of the dive are drawn as stills under the engine's graphics.
- **References for later batches.** Several picks exist only to steer later batches, named under those prompts.

## Midjourney Animate — 34 animations, 33 came back (66 videos)

anabology's brief: "like bliss … we can have midjourney animate for bulk videos that don't need specifications - just movement - and seedance 2.5 for precise videos." Animate turns one image into a five-second video. Here it made the moving windows, the Moon, the dawn, the exteriors and the episode pictures; Seedance did her face and body.

**There are no Animate prompts to quote.** Every animation was made with Midjourney's one-click buttons on the source image's job page (Auto or Loop, Low or High motion); "Animate manually", where a motion prompt is written, was never used. The videos became 1080p frame loops the engine plays (`ui:A-…`), from four lists, `am-anim-1.json` to `am-anim-4.json`.

**Counts** (measured from the files): 34 requested, 33 came back, as **66 MP4s, 573 MB**. Two videos of each came down. All are 1280×720 at 24 fps. The 7 Loops are 121 frames (5.04 s) and seamless; the 26 Auto takes are 125 frames (5.21 s). The Moon-surface animation never came back. 13 animations play in the first cut, 7 in the final film, and A-psumorning lends its camera motion to its still in both. **The videos are not shipped in this package.**
