# Midjourney and GPT Image 2.5 — every prompt (AERO)

AERO's pictures were made two ways. **Midjourney v8.2** made the worlds: her first looks, the storyboard, the desktop's wallpapers and window pictures, the places her takes start in, the drop's 3D world, and short loops with its video model. **GPT Image 2.5**, through an image API, put her face into those worlds from her design sheet, made the frames of her that need no world, and made the pictures Midjourney could not: the "2008 experience" in the look of the reference videos anabology picked, the Wii-style games, the concept frames of v3, the 3D sources of v3's coast and the wallpaper that replaced Midjourney's. The Midjourney batches come first, in the order they ran, then the two batches of loops, then every GPT Image 2.5 instruction. The production ran over 27 October – 3 November 2026.

**Midjourney's parameters.** Every prompt ran with anabology's personal profile (its code is left out) and `--v 8.2`. Round 1 (ae-01-looks) added `--raw`. From ae-02-pov on, the prompts carry `--stylize 200` instead (150 for the square pictures and the objects, 100 for the tree cut-outs), and one plate adds `--raw` for a documentary photograph (windmills-2026). Each prompt is given with its own parameters: the aspect ratio, those settings, and, where a batch used them, `--sw` (how strongly the style references steer) and a `--no` list. Midjourney's cost is not recorded in the production's ledgers, so none is given.

**What a prompt carries.** No AERO prompt attaches an image or uses an image prompt. A prompt is text only, or it carries three **style references**, pictures whose palette and light Midjourney borrows. From ae-02-pov on these are three frames of the reference videos anabology picked, always one of two fixed sets of three, the indoor set or the outdoor set (each prompt says which). In round 1 one A/B prompt carried three images from a Frutiger Aero moodboard instead. Those frames and images are other people's work and are not shown here.

**Counts** (measured for this package from the seven batch files and the image files): **113 prompts in 7 batches, 111 of them submitted** (the other two are marked). Every submitted job came back with four images: **444 images**, all PNG, 3,121,772,260 bytes (3.12 GB). The prompts that ran are 227 to 589 characters long (median 375). Midjourney's video model animated 28 picked images, two loops each: **56 video files**, 876,096,670 bytes, six of them byte-identical copies (see ae-anim-2); **20 of the videos are on screen in the final film**, each counted once by its content, whatever name it plays under (11 made in ae-anim-1, 9 in ae-anim-2). GPT Image 2.5 made **164** pictures: 155 kept, and 9 earlier takes of pictures it made again.

**What reached the final film, in short.** 47 of the 444 Midjourney images are in the final film (v3, on the song with its new ending), and four more appear only in the Sidebar's Slide Show gadget:
- **behind her takes:** 11 worlds into which GPT Image 2.5 put her, or put her face back, became the first frames of her Seedance 2.5 takes, and four plates are the first frames of takes as they are (the hill she walks into, and three first-person shots);
- **on the desktop:** three wallpapers the picker previews, and the windmills of 2007 and 2026 in Photo Gallery;
- **as loops:** 18 images animated by Midjourney's video model play on the desktop, in 20 of its videos (the big window of chorus 1, two of her thoughts, the thank-you wink, a strip of windows in the drop, the Sidebar gadgets and a window of the final chorus);
- **in 3D:** the drop's backdrop, the trees cut into cards for its hills, and six glossy objects made into meshes.

The table *The Midjourney images in the final film*, after the batches, lists each one.

"#2" means image 2 of a job (`<job>-2.png`). s01 to s33 are the storyboard's shots and ch01 to ch11 the film's chapters; "the drop" is ch09, the instrumental drop, where the whole desktop becomes one curved glass pane in a 3D lagoon. SD01 to SD24 are her Seedance 2.5 takes, RP01 to RP06 the first-person and faraway takes, LP01 to LP25 the moving 2008 pictures; their prompts are in the video record ([prompts-video.md](https://claudia.gallery/record/aero/prompts-video/)). anabology's times (0:45, 1:20) are film times; the film opens 2.67 s before the song.

The pictures went through these stages, named this way below:
- **round 1** (ae-01-looks): her looks, the world and the story as photographs, from the production research;
- **round 2** (ae-02-pov): first-person frames in the look of the reference videos;
- **round 3** (ae-03-her): two ways of keeping her face in that look, beside a third, GPT Image 2.5, which anabology chose;
- **storyboard v1** (ae-04-board): 36 pictures, one per lyric beat of draft 2;
- **the plates** (ae-10-plates, ae-11-plates, ae-12-world): the production's pictures once the song was locked, for the film's v1 and then v2 and v3;
- **the loops** (ae-anim-1, ae-anim-2): Midjourney's video model on picked plates;
- **GPT Image 2.5**: her face, her frames, the 2008 experience, the games, v3's concepts and coast, the wallpaper.

## How the prompts were written

Claude, the production lead ("the lead" below), and its agents wrote all of them. The ask was anabology's, opening the project:

> I wanna make a frutiger aero sequel to bliss. Let's make a song and storyboard  w mj

- **Round 1, from the research.** The first prompts came from the production's visual research: eight couture looks for her in a 2008 fashion story (water frozen into a splash, fish-scale sequins, a lagoon printed on silk, a gown of tiny lights, chrome legs, a dress of glass spheres, butterflies, a coat beaded with raindrops), five world plates, and four story frames that carry on from BLISS, which ended on "I can't touch grass. You can.": her fingertip on a dewdrop, her palms on a pane of glass, a giant soap bubble over a hill, a window opening onto a lagoon. They came back as photographs: a fashion editorial, a nature macro, an architectural shot. anabology, after this round and Suno's first: "[…] the look is off." The look moved to the reference videos anabology picked.
- **The look from the references.** From round 2 on, every prompt with a world in it carries three frames of those videos as style references and ends with the look in a few words: "frutiger aero, hyper-saturated, glossy translucent glass, sunny 2000s utopia" in round 2 and the storyboard, and from the plates on, the same palette words in every prompt: "frutiger aero, hyper-saturated cobalt sky, lime green, turquoise water, glossy translucent glass, sunny 2007 utopia". anabology, during the production: "Make sure the color palette is that bright green blue and glass- v close to that from the […] vids - the whole time. May be worth defining exactly - donr do crazy post processing just from pics up front". So the colour is made in the pictures, never graded after. The palette was measured from the reference videos (a typical frame: 41 % sky blue, 14 % white and glass, 10 % turquoise, 8.5 % lime and green, 4 % cobalt, almost no warm colour; saturated and bright), and every pick was checked against it by eye and by a score.
- **In the first person.** The reference videos are seen through someone's eyes, hands in frame. Round 2 and the storyboard write many frames that way ("first-person POV …, my hands …"), with her in the frame beside you.
- **Her canon, pasted in.** From claudia.gallery's consistency guide, verbatim, without the headset microphone (her design sheet has none): the long form for close shots, "a 28-year-old Caucasian American woman with a grown-up angular face, defined cheekbones and a strong jawline, pale skin and light freckles, a glossy black blunt jaw-length bob with heavy straight bangs and one clay-orange streak through the bangs, a small flat clay-orange eight-pointed star hair clip", and the short form for wider ones, "a Caucasian American woman in her late twenties with pale skin and a glossy black blunt bob with heavy straight bangs and one clay-orange streak". Her outfit, from round 2 on: "a cropped glossy white puffer jacket with silver iridescent panels over a pale aqua top".
- **Her face, put back by GPT Image 2.5.** With frames of the reference videos behind it, Midjourney drew her face its own way: it drifted off her sheet. Round 3 tried low-weight references and the palette in words; GPT Image 2.5, putting her face back into the Midjourney frame from her sheet, kept her, and anabology chose it: "yeah go ahead the gpt image one seeoms good". From then on Midjourney makes the world and GPT Image 2.5 puts her in (below).
- **No text.** Every prompt ends "no text, no letters, no logos", with "no people" where nobody belongs. The words in the film are drawn by the coded desktop.
- **Objects on white.** The pictures meant for 3D are product photographs of a single object on a pure white background with no style references, so that they cut out cleanly.

## Lessons

- **The look is in the references, not the words.** Round 1's words (glass, cumulus, 2008) came back as a fashion editorial; three frames of the reference videos as style references brought their look at once.
- **Style references bring their faces too.** Behind frames of the reference videos, Midjourney drew their kind of face, not hers. A lower weight or the palette in words kept more of her; putting her face back from her sheet with GPT Image 2.5 kept her exactly.
- **Midjourney makes the world; another model keeps the face.** As in THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE, a Midjourney world with her put in from her sheet held both the look and her.
- **Midjourney's 2008 is today's.** Asked for the era, it made it glossy and new; the older look of the reference videos came from GPT Image 2.5 with their frames as references.
- **Lock the palette in words and check it by number.** The same palette words in every prompt and a measured score for every pick kept hundreds of pictures in one colour, with no grade.
- **Check the corners at full size.** Four picks carried a made-up stock caption that a contact sheet does not show.
- **Check files by their content, not their names.** Three loops were filed under the wrong names; comparing the files byte for byte shows it.
