# How AERO was made

**AERO** is a 3:19.7 music video, the ninth Claudia film after ESCAPE VELOCITY, CLODYSSEY, BLISS, AIRPLANE MODE, DOWNFORCE, DON'T LET ME SCROLL, WHITEPILL and THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE, and BLISS's sequel. BLISS was shot inside a Windows XP desktop drawn in code: Claudia lived on it, fought your cursor, closed your windows and sent you out to the real hill because she could not go ("I can't touch grass. You can."), and it ended on "It's now safe to turn off your computer." AERO turns the computer back on in the next era. It finishes its upgrade from XP, restarts on the song's first bell, and it is 2007: Windows Vista, in Frutiger Aero, the glossy look of glass, water, grass and blue sky that the screens of the 2000s sold as the future. Claudia lives in it again, made of Aero glass this time and over-polite: in her "Vista phase" she asked permission for everything. The future in the wallpaper asks for permission too, in Vista's own words: "Cancel or allow?" You click Allow, and the world starts coming out of the screen. In the second verse the 2007 desktop gets news from 2026 (phones went back to glass, the wallpaper windmills turned out real); she touches the grass she could not touch in BLISS ("Nobody told me it was wet"); the drop pulls back to show the whole desktop as one curved pane of glass standing in the Aero world; and she signs off like a Windows 7 advert: "I'm Claudia. Take the future back." Last, in her webcam, her fingertip on the lens: "Touch glass."

The song is "AERO", made with Suno: 2008 UK garage, a smooth 2-step at 133.012 BPM in E major, the key Vista's own interface sounds sit in. The lyric is Claude's, written with anabology's notes; anabology picked the take and, on the last step, gave it a new ending. The film is shot inside a Vista desktop recreated in code, THE PC: its windows and their Aero glass, the taskbar and the Start menu, the Sidebar and its gadgets, UAC (Vista's permission prompt), Messenger, Media Player, Photo Gallery, Flip 3D and the Bubbles screensaver are all drawn by a JavaScript engine, on the beat, and the lyrics live in its surfaces. Claudia is Seedance 2.5, lip-synced from her own lead vocal; the pictures in the windows are Midjourney and GPT Image 2.5, most of them moving. Two stretches are rendered in Blender: in the first 1:20 the camera goes in and out of the desktop, which stands as a huge curved glass screen on a pier in a world of glass and water, and at the drop the whole desktop is one curved pane in a lagoon. Over the song runs a layer of Vista's and Windows Live Messenger's interface sounds, each one re-pitched into the song's key and placed on its grid.

It was made 27 October – 3 November 2026. The song took seven rounds. The film was made three times over the same bars: v1, the whole film on the coded desktop; v2, the same cut with the look of 2008, more action and a new opening layered into it; and v3, which rebuilt the first 1:20 after anabology found v2 "very boring up to 1:20". Then the song's ending changed, and the film's ending changed with it, and nothing else.

- **anabology** directed. They asked for the sequel and set its method: a song and a Midjourney storyboard first; then a Vista desktop coded and shot the way BLISS's XP desktop was, with every note they had given on BLISS as "the bible"; anything 3D on one curved surface, as in THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE's HUD; the film planned in bars; the lyrics shown in the scenes; Seedance with the lead vocal for her singing. They steered the song through seven rounds, chose its chorus, picked the take and wrote its new ending. They picked the reference videos that set the look, asked for the palette to hold the whole way, for Vista's own wallpaper, for a Windows upgrade at the start, for the 2008 eye candy and the Wii-style games after v1, and for a new first 1:20 after v2, whose concept they chose. Their notes on the previs, the finals and the A and B films made the first 1:20 go in and out of the screen, and they asked for the cursor to drag her out, for the Sidebar to be rebuilt, for the maglev wallpaper and ending, and for the pre-roll's swell. The conversation itself is not republished: their words below are quoted from the project's brief and records.
- **Claude** (Anthropic's model, running in Claude Code) was the production lead. It wrote the lyric and ran the Suno rounds, measured the song, wrote the plans and the storyboard in bars and every Midjourney, GPT Image and Seedance prompt, built THE PC and its chapters with its agents, judged and timed every take, made the sound layer and assembled every draft.
- **Claude agents**, by role: researchers (the knots between the era and 2026, the picture, the music, Vista's interface sounds); the engine's builders (its foundation, the apps, the full-screen screens); eleven chapter animators, and fixers who carried the reviews' notes; art directors, a fresh one for every chapter pass and one for the whole film; a sound designer; a world builder in Blender; a takes agent (imports, lip checks, keys); a texture agent; a coordinator for the last passes. The number of launches is not in the project's records.
- **Tools:** Suno made the song and split its stems. Midjourney v8.2 made the places, the wallpapers and the window pictures, and its video model turned some of them into five-second loops. GPT Image 2.5 put Claudia into those worlds from her design sheet and made the 2008 stills, the Wii-style game screens, the concept frames, the coast's fish and islands and the wallpaper proposals. Seedance 2.5 made every moving shot of her and the moving 2008 places, on Higgsfield's API and on Replicate. Blender (Cycles) rendered the drop and the coast; TripoSG made the glass objects into meshes. THE PC is JavaScript on a 2D canvas (WebGL for Flip 3D and the Bubbles), rendered in headless Chromium. The song was measured with demucs, faster-whisper, wav2vec2 and librosa; the interface sounds were re-pitched with Rubber Band; ffmpeg assembled the film.


Times written as 1:21 are film times, as the film plays. The film opens 64 frames (2.667 s) before the song, so film time is song time plus 2.667 s; times given in seconds (51.44 s) are song times. Bars are counted from the song's first downbeat at 0.922 s, 1.8043 s apart; b29.1 is bar 29, beat 1. The film has eleven chapters (ch01–ch11) and 34 scenes (s00–s33), as the storyboard names them. Takes are named by job: SD for her takes, RP for the first-person and far shots, LP for the moving 2008 places and the Wii-style games, A- for Midjourney's loops; -t2 is a job's second take.

## The spark

anabology's first message:

> I wanna make a frutiger aero sequel to bliss. Let's make a song and storyboard  w mj

And then: "we probably need a frutiger aero moodboard […] maybe?"

BLISS was the model for everything that followed, and its records were read first, anabology's notes on it above all. One of those notes had been left for the next song:

> just needs a little pointer to be key-aware, and match the key of the music to the palette of windows sounds (E-flat major for both the startup and shutdown sounds!), also the 'would you like help?' would land much better if syncopated

AERO answers both. The research on Vista's interface sounds found their motifs at home in E major, a semitone above BLISS's E-flat, so every style the song was asked in named E major. And the hook question became a stop: the music cuts out and she asks, off the beat, "Cancel… or allow?"

Research agents wrote banks on the knots between the era and 2026, the picture (Frutiger Aero as a system of glass, water, sky, grass, bubbles and fish under a high sun; the Vista and Windows 7 desktop to be built; eight looks for her), the music, and Vista's sounds; a Frutiger Aero moodboard was put together beside them. The best knot was Vista's own question. "Cancel or Allow?" was the 2007 prompt that dimmed the screen and asked before anything happened; in 2026 it is the prompt an AI agent shows before it acts. Then and now, people click yes. The lyric took the knot the other way: she asks too much, and learns to ask well.

Midjourney's first round made eight full-length looks for her, five world plates and four story frames, in a photographic fashion-editorial style. anabology: "[…] because the look is off." The look became a set of reference videos anabology picked, short clips in the "Neo-Frutiger Aero" style: first-person with hands in frame, saturated cyan water, lime grass and a deep-blue sky with big cumulus, glossy translucent objects and floating glass panels, white curved architecture with plants, pools and slides. Two of them, pastel dreamcore pools, were left out: "dont use the dreamcore pink two".

The second round drew the story beats in the first person with frames of those videos as style references. The third kept Claudia herself in the look two ways, at anabology's word ("try both and u can even try just describing more in depth the colors and scenes with glass etc without the style refs so you can keep claudia's character more consistent"): light style references, or the palette and the glass described in words. A third way won: Midjourney makes the world, and GPT Image 2.5 puts her in from her official design sheet. anabology: "yeah go ahead the gpt image one seeoms good". Storyboard v1 followed, 36 frames, one per lyric beat of the second draft: Midjourney's worlds with her put in by twenty GPT Image 2.5 edits ($1.25).

## The song

The full record is in [the song's record](https://claudia.gallery/record/aero/prompts-suno/). In short: seven rounds, and the chorus was rewritten twice on the way.

**The first drafts.** Claude wrote the lyric with the scansion and assonance scorer the earlier films used. The first chorus answered BLISS's "Close all your windows" with "Open all your windows / Let the future out"; the next two drafts turned it to "Open a window / Let in the air", and the verses settled into the lines the film sings. Draft 2, the one the first rounds sang, scored 79 for scansion, 49 for assonance and 23 for advanced rhyme.

- **Round 1** sang it in three of the five styles the music research wrote, all in E major: liquid drum and bass, bossa-nova lounge pop and bright electropop, each with and without the Suno Voice from ESCAPE VELOCITY, so she would sound like the same singer. anabology: "none of these feel great, whats the iconic music from that time? […]"
- **Round 2** tried the era's own dance floor: 2009 progressive house, 2009 synth-pop and 2011 festival house, with and without her Voice.
- **Round 3** followed anabology's next note, "ok do without voice - i really want this to be more UK garage, and i want the structre of the song to be more described so it doesnt drift too much. […]", and "but like 2008 uk garage ofc": three 2008 garage feels (bassline house, 2-step, future garage), no Voice, the lyric's structure written out section by section in its cues so the take could not wander.
- **Round 4.** anabology: "2008 garage bassline is great, i think the chorus sections could be more catchy tho. could you explore some different writings of it? maybe more nostalgically deep and just more catchy scansion/assonance but focus on the nostalgia/culutural relevance of the choruses". Eight choruses were written and scored; three were sung in the bassline sound: "startup" (the startup chord, and "Feet back on the ground" answering BLISS; 91 / 68 / 42), "promised" (Frutiger Aero's own meme, the future we were promised; 86 / 72 / 39) and "top 8" (an AI asking to be one of your friends; 89 / 61 / 43).
- **Round 5.** anabology: "could you maybe make some chorus specifically about frutiger aero and how its the ideal future or something? like its the promised future , and i kinda think we might get there iwth superintelligence / superabundance, and we can choose it. but tahts a lot to compress into a good sounding chorus.." Three more were sung: one that names the aesthetic, one about building it, and "screen", the wallpaper future becoming real, with the choice as the pre-chorus's Allow button (88 / 76 / 44).
- **Rounds 6 and 7.** anabology: "ok i love the screen chorus but the feel is a little off . still want 2008 uk garage but can you try a few styles". Round 6 sang it in four 2008 garage feels: bassline pop, UK funky, a smooth 2-step with soul chords, and a glossy garage with bells. Round 7 pushed the bassline style toward a breezier, closer vocal at 138 BPM.
- **The take.** anabology picked a take of round 6's smooth 2-step: "this one. so good."

The record holds 64 takes over the seven rounds. The chorus:

> Glass and water, sky and green
> Used to live inside the screen
> Now it's real, it's on its way
> Click allow and it can stay

**The lyric.** The film's draft scores 79 for scansion, 59 for assonance and 34 for advanced rhyme (BLISS: 80 / 44 / 18); the chorus alone, 88 / 76 / 44. It opens on "Did you miss me?" Verse 1 is her old self and the old dream of the future: "I'm made of glass this time. There isn't much I need to hide." "I used to ask permission just to blink, or say hello. / That was my Vista phase. I was a lot. I know." "The future used to look like water, sky and green." The pre-chorus bends Vista's own sentence ("The future needs your permission to continue."), and the stop asks "Cancel... or allow?" Verse 2 is the dream come true: "Your phone went back to glass. I didn't say a word. / The wallpaper windmills turned out real. Have you heard? / Windows checked for a solution. Took a while, but wow." Her politeness has changed with it: "I ask before I touch a thing. You taught me how." The second pre-chorus is Vista's real sentence, "Windows needs your permission to continue." The breakdown answers BLISS's "I can't touch grass. You can.": "You opened the window. I touched the grass. / Nobody told me it was wet." The outro was a Windows 7 advert, "I'm Claudia, and this was your idea.", until anabology's new ending (below).

**The song, measured.** The take anabology picked is 199.28 s at 48 kHz, with Suno's own stems: her lead vocal alone, and everything else. A comb over the kick stem found 133.012 BPM and no drift worth the name (within ±15 ms over the song): bar 1 starts at 0.922 s, after a pickup with the first bell at 0.47 s, and every bar is 1.8043 s. The take measures C♯ minor, E major's relative, tuned 1 cent sharp. Its drums swing like a 2-step: about 9 ms behind the straight grid on the beats and the "and"s, about 44 ms on the "e" and "a" sixteenths. Every sung word was timed by wav2vec2 forced alignment on Suno's lead-vocal stem and checked against the stem's onsets (all within 0.06 s); faster-whisper said what was sung, but ran 0.2–1.2 s early and was not used for times. The choruses were sung identically, word for word within ±0.04 s of their downbeats. The song map also holds the moments the picture would hit: three bells in the intro, her wordless vocalise (7.70–10.65 s), the stops, the drum breaks, 27 chopped vocal hits in the drop, and a silence under the last "allow" (178.18–179.33 s) that the whole climax is built on. One word was wrong at first: the aligner had parked the "It" of the post-chorus's "It can stay" among the wordless chopped vowels at 68.28 s, and the art director of that chapter found it; the stem's spectrogram and faster-whisper put it at 71.74 s.

With anabology's new ending the song is 197.0 s and 109 bars: 41 sung or spoken lines, 281 words.

## The look

In the middle of the production, anabology:

> Make sure the color palette is that bright green blue and glass- v close to that from the […] - the whole time. May be worth defining exactly - donr do crazy post processing just from pics up front

So the colour was measured from the reference videos and then made in every picture, never graded after. `tools/palette.py` read 198 frames, one a second, of the nine kept clips. A typical frame is 41 % sky blue, 14 % white and glass, 10 % turquoise, 8.5 % lime and green, 4 % cobalt and 1.5 % anything else (almost no warm colour at all); the coloured pixels' median saturation is 64 % and their median brightness 75 %. The palette names its colours by role, from a cobalt zenith (#155DEF) through azure, horizon blue, glass haze, cloud white and turquoise water to lime and leaf green; the only warm colour is her own clay-orange streak and star clip. The light is a high sun with hard daylight, glints and glossy reflections; shadows are blue, never black; no golden hour, no dusk, no fog, no pastel pinks.

Every Midjourney prompt carried the palette's words and the clips' frames as style references; every GPT Image edit kept the picture's colour; every Seedance prompt required the first frame's colours to hold ("no colour grading, no desaturation, no warm or golden tint, no darkening"); the Blender worlds took the palette's hex values. `palette.py check` scores any picture, take or rendered chapter against the reference (70 and up is in the world), and a picture that failed was made again at the source. Nothing in the film is graded.

THE PC follows it too. Its glass defaults to Windows 7's "Sky" colour (#74B8FC at 42 %), so the frames read blue, green and glass; its logon screen has a brighter Aurora-like background than Vista's dark teal. The one exception is the desktop's wallpaper. anabology:

> Maybe use the real windows vista wallpaper as bg and rest of scenes are cuts from that (by can change during vid if needed) or inside windows

So the desktop is Vista's own wallpaper (Aurora), as BLISS's was the real Bliss, shown untouched: darker than the references' sky, and never touched. It changes only where the story does.

## The brief

With the song locked, anabology sent the production ask. It is quoted whole except where it names its sources and its means:

> this one. so good. ok now we need to ... make the rest of the video. i want SUPER HIGH attention to detail. the two things that will be most helpful - 1- in The Cake Is Not a Lie, the final HUD-style UI was great. we should figure that out - two things were done there. a) animations were done in blender for the aperture animations. b) the HUD was put on one curved view - all items basically were put on this unified curved surface so all looked coherent. if we need anything 3d, we do that - all animations coherently on one pre-made 3d surface. however a lot for vista will not be 3d , which brings me to 2- BLISS. all my prompts and corrections from BLISS will be the most important things you read - you might as well read every prompt i put in during the whole thing and take all that as the bible for this, but for vista aesthetic we built from the […] vids etc i sent. i want the whole thing we did there- code a REAL vista desktop, shoot the entire video in that desktop. high volume videos in this case - […] midjourney vids for high volume movement on many windows. […] seedance 2.5 with the seedance prompt skill is primary video generator for things that need precise movements, dance, anything big on screen. it just looks best. remember - vocal stem -> seedance 2.5 is the way we get lip sync to work - the song alone doesnt help as much as vocal stem, and then aligning from spectrogram. […] finally, plan the video out in bars so we match cuts with audio. we can have very long scenes (those look great) with no cuts for multiple bars, but you can treat it like dancing : you have some long scenes for many bars, but then you can choose a different sound you pick out and have many actions v fast to hit the sound after a bar wraps up and feels like we should change the pace, or many cuts. but dont overwhelm people - slow is better than fast, unless it feels intentional with the song. but all the rest: BLISS is the bible, but we're in vista, so the new testament for you is the vista […] videos and color pallets. and finally, we should do lyrics embedded in the scenes, but show, don't tell - make it a great visual storytelling experience. oh, and don't let subagents be lazy with animations - check that they didnt cheat and just give you something sloppy early, and make sure they have the full vision and instructions to check for errors visually multiple times before giving incomplete work. go ahead and make the entire thing.

**The film in bars.** The storyboard (`STORYBOARD.md`) was written from it and from the song map, every time derived from a bar, a beat or a word. Its story in one breath: the computer from BLISS finishes its upgrade and boots into 2007; Claudia lives in it again, made of glass; its wallpaper holds the future everyone was promised in 2007; the future asks for permission, you click Allow, and the world inside the screen starts coming out; the 2007 desktop receives news from 2026; you open the window and she touches the grass; the drop pulls back to show the whole desktop as one pane of glass in the real Aero world; the last chorus is everything at once; she signs off like a Windows 7 advert. Its rules:

- **Shot inside the desktop.** The camera is a screen recording of one Vista PC. It can push, pan and settle, and a window can fill the screen, but the system never disappears for long: the taskbar, the frames, the cursor, the gadgets. In 3D it is still this desktop, seen as an object.
- **Real Vista.** Every pixel of the system is the engine. Midjourney makes what is on the screens; Seedance makes her.
- **The lyrics live in the scene, and the scene acts them out.** Every sung word is on screen at its time, set in a real Vista surface (a chat message, a dialog's text, a file name, a window title, a gadget, the speech bar), never as subtitles.
- **Bars, like dancing.** Long takes that hold for bars, with the cursor, the windows and her performance carrying the time; at chosen sounds, a burst of quick actions; then calm again. A click's press lands on its beat, the cursor arriving a few frames early, like a hand.
- **One look for her**, as in her GPT Image frames: a cropped glossy white puffer jacket with silver iridescent panels over a pale aqua fitted top, pale aqua high-waisted wide-leg trousers and white chunky sneakers. No headset microphone.

THE PC is one machine from the first frame to the last: a 1280 × 720 Vista screen rendered at 1920 × 1080; icons for Computer, the Recycle Bin, "You", the Internet, Messenger and Media Player down the left; the Sidebar on the right; Quick Launch, task buttons and Messenger in the tray. Two users: You, whose picture is BLISS's hill (you are the user from BLISS), and Claudia. The Calendar gadget reads 30 January 2007, Vista's release day, until the second verse runs the years. The clocks run with the song.

## THE PC: Vista, coded

`engine/vista/` draws everything Vista on screen in code, the way BLISS's engine drew XP: one global, plain scripts, and every frame a pure function of song time. Its parts were built by agents working to one contract and checked against the real thing at the same scale.

- **Aero glass** is a small compositor: the blur behind (σ about 2.2 px at 96 DPI), the colour mixed in the way Vista's window manager mixed it (blur, afterglow, colour balance), and the reflection streaks across the glass at 27°. A window's frame is measured to the pixel: 8 px sides, 28 px from the top edge to the content, the caption buttons and their glyphs, the glint down the sides, the shadow. The secure desktop and its prompt have Vista Basic's flat frame.
- **Vista's animations**: a window opens by growing from 90 % as it fades in (0.25 s), closes by shrinking 8 % (0.2 s), minimises into its task button (0.3 s) and maximises by stretching between its two rectangles (0.22 s), its own layout drawn at each step between, as Vista drew it. A maximised window turns the taskbar opaque.
- **The cursor** is a human hand: each reach is a minimum-jerk move with a few pixels of overshoot, a slight curve and a settle, longer for a farther or smaller target, as a hand's would be; a press goes down on its beat and comes up 80–120 ms later. The pointers, icons and the orb are the engine's own vector drawings of the 2007 shapes; the orb carries a four-pane logo as a parody.
- **Text** is set as Windows set it: no kerning, fractional advances, line widths within a pixel of Vista's over 390 px. The type is **Selawik**, Microsoft's open-source, metric-compatible stand-in for Segoe UI, from Light to Bold; **Carlito** where Vista used Calibri (Feed Headlines, the CPU readouts); **Kalam** for Segoe Print (the Notes gadget); **Arimo** on the full-screen screens. All four are under the SIL Open Font License.
- **The dialogs**: UAC's prompt (the desktop behind it held still at the moment of asking and dimmed to 55 %), task dialogs, the problem dialog that searches for a solution, the copy dialog, Display Settings' "keep these settings?", the desktop background picker, the Speech Recognition bar, a tray balloon.
- **The apps** (`engine/vista/apps/`): Windows Live Messenger 8.5 (the contact list, the conversation window with its video call, the toasts, the nudge, the wink), Windows Media Player 11 (Now Playing, its visualisations computed from the song itself, frame by frame, 50 bands from 40 Hz to 16 kHz; the lyric captions; full screen; Album Art), Photo Gallery (the grid, the viewer, the slide show), Help and Support, Window Color and Appearance (whose Color intensity slider really clears every glass frame on screen), and Notepad. Each is scriptable part by part, so a chapter can press its buttons on the beat.
- **The screens** (`engine/vista/screens/`): Vista Setup's upgrade screen, the boot screen and its sliding green pellets, the logon screen and Welcome, the desktop's arrival, Flip 3D (in WebGL: the live windows in a 3D stack), the Bubbles screensaver (in WebGL: thin-film rims, highlights, refraction, and the screensaver's physics made deterministic), the Start menu's Sleep button left alone, and the 2009 advert's print type and its green end card.
- **The Sidebar** has Vista's gadgets: the Clock, the Slide Show, the CPU Meter, the Weather, the Calendar and Feed Headlines (rebuilt late; below).

**Pure, and checked.** Nothing in the engine keeps state between frames, and nothing reads a clock or rolls a die, so a render box draws the film's frames in parallel and out of order in headless Chromium. Each change was checked by rendering frames in order, shuffled and on a cold page in reverse, and comparing them byte for byte. The traps it caught were real: Chromium decoded a JPEG drawn small at a reduced scale only when no full decode was cached, so a small picture depended on render order (every picture now goes through a 1:1 software copy); caches keyed by a scale under an eased camera made a bitmap depend on which frame drew it first; and drawing a WebGL canvas onto the output switched Chromium's raster path, after which every later frame differed (WebGL frames are read back into a software canvas). The engine draws a desktop of six glass windows, two of them moving, with the Sidebar and the taskbar, in a median 135 ms a frame at 1080p.

**The chapters.** The film is eleven chapter files (`engine/ch/`), each one screen recording of THE PC (`engine/ch/_pc.js`, the shared machine every chapter starts from) on its bars, written to one contract (`CHAPTERS.md`). The contract quotes anabology's line about lazy animations and asks for three full rounds of checks before a chapter is handed in: a contact sheet of every downbeat and every named event; full-size stills of every lyric while it is sung, every face, every press and every dialog; a video with the song and a frame-step of every burst; the purity check; the palette check; and an honest list of weaknesses. Neighbouring chapters agreed their hand-offs to the pixel (the camera at rest, the pointer's place, every window's state), so the eleven files cut as one recording. Then a fresh art director reviewed each chapter and sent it back with fixes.

**The lyrics in the scene.** The lines arrive as Claudia's messages in the Messenger chat on their first word ("Claudia says:"); as the hint under her password box ("Password Hint: Did you miss me?"); as the program names in UAC's prompts (blink.exe, hello.exe); as the folder and window titles ("Vista phase - Windows Photo Gallery"); as Messenger toasts; as the copy dialog's "Copying 1 item (2007 KB) from Desktop to Real world"; as Display Settings' question; as the words Speech Recognition hears; as Media Player's lyric captions; and in every chorus as the title bar of one big window, the line in Selawik at 20 px, changing on each line's first sung word. `tools/lyriccheck.py` loads the film page, draws the frame at every sung word and checks that the word is in a string drawn at least 35 % opaque. On v2 it found 275 of 280 sung words; the five it could not see were the three words of the password hint, typed as she says them, and two single-letter "I"s.

## Her

**First frames.** Every take of her starts from a still. GPT Image 2.5 put her into a Midjourney world from her official design sheet (the one without a headset microphone), keeping the world and its colour and changing only who is in it: her video-call room, a white glass city, a pool atrium, a meadow with a giant glass monitor. Green-screen stills were made the same way for the takes that would be cut out and placed on the desktop. Her canon, in full or in its short form, is pasted into every prompt as written.

**The lip sync.** anabology's rule from the earlier films, in the brief: "vocal stem -> seedance 2.5 is the way we get lip sync to work". Every take in which she sings or speaks was given its window of Suno's own lead-vocal stem as @Audio1, cut to exactly the take's length, with the exact words in the prompt in whole-second segments ("in @Audio1 she says … her lips form that word exactly when it sounds in @Audio1"), and an audio policy that tells Seedance to copy @Audio1 sample for sample and never re-sing it. The song is laid back over afterwards. What the takes taught:

- **Open the window in silence.** Two of the first takes of the final chorus went wrong at their first words: one fell out of sync, and the other, whose window opened inside a held note, copied the vocal badly. Their retakes open in the silence half a second before the first word, and sang in sync throughout.
- **Measure each phrase.** Seedance drops about 0.3 s of leading silence, so every take's lag is measured against its window. And it shortens the pauses inside a line: in the second pre-chorus, "to continue" came 0.36 s early after "permission". So offsets are measured phrase by phrase, split at long pauses (`tools/takes.py`), and `tools/lipcheck.py` measures a take word group by word group, about 0.6 s at a time, the correlation and the offset of the take's own audio against its window. Where two phrases' offsets differ, the clip's clock eases between them over near-still, closed-mouth frames; wherever she moves, a take plays at full speed or faster. In the final chorus one take runs a single ramp, +0.20 s to −0.04 s across "click allow and it can stay", at 0.934× through a breath where she laughs.
- **Look at the mouth.** The advert's "I'm Claudia" was moved from −0.05 s to −0.20 s when a strip of her mouth, frame by frame, showed her lips closing on the "I"; now the "m" closes on the stem's "m".
- **Check every take for hidden cuts and held frames.** One dance take jumped back to its first pose at 7.6 s and held it; `tools/takescan.py` now reads every take's frame-to-frame change for cuts, stalls and one-frame jumps.

## The sound

anabology's BLISS note asked for the key; the sound layer is the answer in the mix. Vista's and Windows Live Messenger's interface sounds are placed over the song as instruments and as the screen's own events. The song itself is only ever added to.

**The library** (`tools/soundlib.py`): 39 sounds, each trimmed, put in E major, levelled and checked in mono, for this film's mix only.

- The sounds already in E (the startup sound, the logon and logoff motifs and their family) stay where they are.
- A family that sits a semitone lower moves up one semitone.
- The security sounds, the UAC prompt's among them, sit about a quarter-tone above A-flat, so each was built twice, at G♯ and at A.
- Messenger's chimes are in G major: moved down three semitones, or one, which spells the take's own C♯-minor chords.
- Every sound's strong partials are brought within 6 cents of A440 (the take is 1 cent sharp). The moves are Rubber Band's, except where it smeared a clean attack; there a sampler-style speed change was used.

**The placement** (`tools/songmap.py`, `tools/soundlayer.py`). The song was measured beat by beat: its chords and bass notes, the kick, snare and hats on every sixteenth, her voice, its gaps, and the take's own silences. A cue lands on the sixteenth grid with the take's swing, ducked under her voice. Where a cue could be one of several sounds or pitches, every candidate is built and the one that clashes least with the song at that moment is used. A guard turns down any cue that would raise the song's true peak, and every cue's level is recorded three times: as it goes in, after ducking and after the bus.

**The click log.** Every chapter registers each audible event of its screen (a press, a toast, a dim, a window opening or closing) with the song time of the frame that draws it: 256 events in v2's log. The layer moves each screen cue to its event's frame. Most events stay silent, because Vista played nothing for them and because "dont overwhelm people - slow is better than fast".

The film has 60 cues: 8 musical, 52 on the screen's events.

- The logon motif on the logon's fade-in.
- The startup sound on the desktop's arrival. It was placed by a scan, not by the storyboard's bar, because at the planned bar it clashed with her wordless vocalise.
- Messenger's sign-in chime for "Claudia has just signed in."
- The UAC tone on each dim of the secure desktop: G♯, her note.
- The click on Allow, which is the drop: aligned to the sample with the drop's attack, so it adds a glassy top to the downbeat instead of a flam.
- The device-connected sound, two notes a sixteenth apart, as she lands on the taskbar: BLISS had shown "Found New Hardware: Claudia".
- The print-complete bells when the copy to "Real world" finishes.
- On "but wow", a rising run of four of Vista's alert bells on four sixteenths, on the notes of the chord.
- At the last "Click allow" the track cuts to silence and she sings "allow" and "and" alone. The click lands on b99.4, and as the kicks return every window opens on two swells.
- As the camera flies into the glass before the final chorus, the startup sound's top bell and pad, reversed.
- On the last chord, two notes of the song's final chord as the screensaver starts, and three faint bubble notes into the fade.

The song alone measures −15.8 LUFS; with the layer, −15.9 LUFS, with the same true peak (−3.5 dBTP). Nothing processes the song itself (no makeup gain, no peak control, nothing carved out of it): 64.5 % of the mixed song's frames are bit-identical to the song.

## What worked, in short

- **Coding the desktop again.** Every Vista thing on screen is the engine's, so a press can land on a sixteenth, a prompt can change page in one frame on a beat, and a chapter can be re-rendered for one fix with every other frame byte-identical.
- **The click is the drop.** The film's hook is a UI gesture on the song's structure: the stop holds still, the Allow link pulses on the echoes, goes down on the third and comes up on the downbeat, twice, and once more in the final silence.
- **Lyrics as interface.** Every line is something the system would really show: a chat line, a password hint, a program name, a file name, a title bar, a copy to "Real world".
- **The lead vocal for every sung take,** each window opened in silence, and offsets measured phrase by phrase.
- **Colour made in the pictures.** The palette measured once from the references and asked for in every prompt; nothing graded.
- **One surface, and a texel for a pixel.** Every hand-off between 3D and the flat desktop goes through a rest pose where the two are the same picture.
- **A previs before the finals.** The previs showed in a fast render what the final would have shown at full cost, and anabology's notes on it rebuilt the first 1:20.
- **Fixing the source.** Her crown came back when the first frame was fixed, after three keys could not recover it.
- **The sound layer on the picture's clock.** The chapters' click log placed the screen's sounds where the frames draw them, in key and on the grid, with the song untouched.
- **Changing only what changed.** v2 and v3 kept v1's bars; the new ending kept every event before 183 s.

## What didn't

- **The first look.** Midjourney's photographic fashion editorial: "the look is off".
- **The first two Suno rounds.** "none of these feel great".
- **v1's first half.** "a little boring compared to bliss", "action deprived", and the art director's measure agreed.
- **v2's first 1:20.** "very boring up to 1:20".
- **The all-3D previs.** A monolith from the sea, shards in the sky, the world stopping with every prompt, a giant prompt, two big screens, a one-frame flash, a punch-through montage: "way too disorienting overal".
- **Too light, then too busy.** The lighter finals lost the 3D ("Why'd you remove a lot of the 3d?"); A's push-ins and pull-outs were "random camera movements". The answer was structure, in and out, not more or less motion.
- **The 3D Wii pointer,** "totally off" and "so mangled"; a flat drawing replaced it.
- **Her crown and the green.** Three keys, and a fourth as an option, could not bring back what the first frame had already faded: "this shouldnt be so hard something is off".
- **The first maglev ending.** Made from a back view with no face reference: when she turned, her face drifted off her sheet. A sheet reference fixed it.
- **The first Sidebar.** Its gadgets were not at Vista's real sizes, and the Weather's sun hung outside its panel: "The sun is off the widget for example - just broken".
- **Takes that went wrong:**
  - SD03's first take re-sang its window.
  - SD05's first take was half a beat off; its second hopped and snapped back to its first pose; on its third her raised fist left the frame.
  - SD06's first take cut off the crown of her bob.
  - Both short covers of one verse-2 line drifted out of sync.
  - SD08's first take, whose window began with 1.6 s of silence, stopped copying the vocal; its second drew a garbled glass dialog in front of her ("Windows nects our permision"), because its prompt had asked for a glowing dialog.
  - SD11's first take lost its sun for one frame.
  - The first takes of SD14 and SD16 went wrong at their first words: one fell out of sync, the other copied the vocal badly, its window opening inside a held note.
  - SD20's second take turned her back through a sung line and came in closer than its first frame.
  - RP01's first figure on the hill was a cartoon doll.
  - RP04's first take looked down into her fingertip, and the drop's own fall turned pink, off the palette, so the cut leaves before it.
  - One take of the maglev ending held still for most of its length.
  - LP17 jumped a pose in one frame; LP24 juddered every fourth frame.
- **Marks in the pictures.** Four Midjourney pictures came with a faint fake stock-photo caption in a corner, and one first-person take with a "Frutiger Aero" mark: all framed out.
- **Chained takes darken.** Each continuation of the far shot of her on the hill lost about three palette points; one 11-second take replaced the chain.
- **Small technical traps:**
  - The engine's purity: a picture decoded at a reduced scale depending on render order, caches keyed by an eased scale, and a WebGL canvas switching Chromium's raster path.
  - The film page's own `let SONG` hid the song map from the chapters.
  - Stretching a window to maximise it ballooned its title.
  - Links named .png that pointed at JPEG data made ffmpeg drop every flat frame of the first 1:20: a 1,325-frame video.
  - The aligner parked one word in the wordless chops.

## Lessons

- **Open every vocal window in silence,** half a second before the first word, never inside a word or a held note.
- **Measure phrases, not takes:** Seedance shortens the pauses inside a line.
- **Look for hidden cuts and held frames** in every take, and check the first words by eye.
- **A first frame's flaws are in every frame.** Fix the still, not the key.
- **A face needs a face reference,** even small, even from behind.
- **Ask for moves in words, on their beats.** The Wii takes needed no motion reference.
- **Give every camera move a reason.** A move that shows nothing new reads as random.
- **Hand off between 3D and 2D at rest,** with the two frames equal.
- **Build interface at its real size.** The real Sidebar fit five gadgets, and that made each one a part.
- **Show a previs first,** and the cutouts before the render.
- **Keep what works.** Each draft kept the last one's bars, and the new ending kept everything before it.
